My research examines how people build trust and gain influence in complex organizational and social settings. These interactions involve competing interests, uncertain intentions, and dependence on others’ cooperation. I study how emotions, communication, and judgments of trustworthiness shape people’s ability to advance their goals and sustain productive relationships. Negotiation provides a central setting for this work because its consequences extend beyond the agreement itself to implementation, future exchange, and the development or erosion of trust.

Across my studies, I examine how attempts to influence others affect trust, how people interpret and respond to damaged relationships, and how negotiators pursue economic goals while attending to their counterpart’s experience. My current work extends these questions to interactions with AI, where the meaning of trust and the relational value of negotiation take new forms.

Selected Recent Publications

Is There a Human Premium in Negotiations? A Relational-Economic Tradeoff in Human vs. AI Bargaining
Group Decision and Negotiation, in press. Examines how negotiating with a human rather than an AI counterpart may affect relational and economic outcomes.

Hidden Dissatisfaction After Agreement: Post-Negotiation Perceptual Alignment
Current Opinion in Psychology, 2027; published online August 27, 2026. Develops a framework for understanding why negotiators can reach agreement while misunderstanding how the other person experienced the interaction.

A Call for Transdisciplinary Trust Research in the Artificial Intelligence Era
Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 2025. Establishes a cross-disciplinary research agenda for understanding and building trust in artificial intelligence. Published in a Nature Portfolio journal.

When Women Ask, Does Curiosity Help?
Social Sciences, 2024. Shows how curiosity can support successful negotiation outcomes without imposing the same relational costs often associated with women’s assertiveness.

Retrospective Blind Spots in Reputation Management
Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 2023. Explains how later transgressions can change the way observers interpret earlier positive behavior.

The (Mostly) Robust Influence of Initial Trustworthiness Beliefs
Human Relations, 2022. Examines how initial trust judgments shape later behavior and perceptions, including after trust violations.

Motivated by Guilt and Low Felt Trust
Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 2019. Shows how emotional tactics used during negotiation can affect trust and cooperation during implementation.
